How Daemon Works

Daemon is a twisted take on 5e set in Night City. Every merc is a 5e Fighter and keeps all of its normal Fighter features.

There are no classes and no feats. Every merc fills a role instead. What really makes your merc yours is the perk trees you climb with your stats, ported from Cyberpunk 2077.

Skill proficiencies

Proficiencies are unlockable. The skill proficiency is available when any of your stats reach a higher tier. If several stats qualify, you choose ONE skill among all of them, and the rest stay locked until each stat reaches its next tier. You only get one pick per level. New Mercs don't get one at level 1, and their starting stats all count toward the pick on their first level up.

For example: A Bruiser with STR 15, TEC 12 and INT 10 picks Athletics, so TEC and INT give nothing until they hit 14.

Double proficiencies are also attainable, but only once the stat containing the skill you wish to have double proficiency in moves TWO tiers up.

For Example: A Rookie Netrunner proficient in Hacking can't double their proficiency in Hacking until they've reached the Phenom tier in INT.

Your Turn

Normal 5e gives you a movement, an action and a bonus action. Daemon gives you 2 actions per turn to spend however you want.

As part of your MOVE action, if you move 15 ft in a straight line you can LEAP. Pick one.

Attacks and Weapons

Rate of fire

Every weapon has a Rate of Fire, or ROF, which varies weapon to weapon. ROF is how many times you can attack with that weapon on one ATTACK action. A 3-ROF weapon makes 3 separate d20 rolls to HIT.

Weapon chart

Every weapon's ROF and damage at each tier. Tier bonuses stack. Each tier keeps what the tiers below it give, so the columns show the full damage at that tier.

GunsMelee
Tier 1Base diceBase dice + stat mod
Tier 2+1 damage+1 damage
Tier 3+ the stat mod (REF or STR)+1 more damage, so +2
Tier 4+1 more damage, so +2, and a unique effect+1d4 and a unique effect

Example. A Tier 4 Light Pistol does 2d6 + 2 + REF mod piercing. A Tier 4 Sword does 2d6 + 1d4 + 2 + REF mod slashing. The Tier 4 effect is different for every weapon, and the DM sets it. Your sheet does the math with your own mods on equipped weapons.

Concealment

Pistols, Revolvers, Hand Cannons and SMGs can be concealed, and so can some Assault Rifles. The DM decides which ones. A gun can only be concealed with a Standard clip. Swap in an Extended or Drum clip and everyone can see it.

Quick reload

With double proficiency in Sleight of Hand, you can sacrifice 1 ROF to RELOAD instead of spending a whole ACTION on it. A Soldier with a 3-ROF weapon and double proficiency in Sleight of Hand reloads by making their next ATTACK action 2-ROF.

Every gun on your sheet shows its ROF, reload speed, clip and whether it's concealable. Equipped guns track the shots you have left. Tap Fire to spend one ATTACK's worth and Reload to fill the clip. The DM sets these, and you can edit them in the item.

RAM

RAM is the fuel for reactions, hacks and some perks. Your max RAM is 4 + your INT mod, plus whatever your installed cyberware adds.

The INT perk Extra Slot doubles your max RAM. Automation cuts every RAM cost by 1, so reactions only cost 1.

Overheated

If your RAM hits 0 your system overheats. You're stunned and can't do anything until your RAM comes back. The INT perk Insulated Wires keeps you from ever overheating. The DM can drain or restore your RAM at any time.

Cooldown round

A cooldown round is a round where you use no RAM. After one, all your RAM comes back.

Quickhacks

Quickhacks are actions you pay for with RAM. All of your RAM comes back after a cooldown round where you use none.

The INT perk Automation cuts every RAM cost by 1. Entries marked TBD are waiting on the GM.

Netrunning

The Net is a space of opportunity and terror, and Netrunners take it on head first. To go in you need the INT perk Jack In. Here's what every rookie Netrunner should know before hacking a network.

Access Points

To get into a network you need a place to Jack In, called an Access Point. Use the SCAN skill to find one, which costs an ACTION. Once you've found it and reached it, you can Jack In. If you do that on the same turn as the SCAN, it costs nothing extra. On a later turn, Jacking In costs an ACTION.

Diving during combat

  • Netrunning happens on your normal turn in initiative. Net actions use the same ACTIONS as everything else.
  • You still control your IRL body while you're under, so you can defend yourself or hide. Each turn you decide whether to dive deeper into The Net or fight the gonks with your team.
  • Two or more Netrunners can dive at the same time, each on their own turn. To assist another runner with a hack, CODE or Net combat, spend one or both of your ACTIONS on it.
  • Stay within 15 ft of the access point. If you're moved or knocked outside that range mid-dive, you take an Unsafe Jack-Out.

In The Net

A network is built like a building with several floors. Each floor holds an obstacle or a reward. When you get in you'll meet a Firewall, Black ICE, a Daemon or nothing at all.

While you're in, you can do a SCAN Ping. Spend 1 RAM and roll a SCAN check. Depending on the roll, you learn how many floors the network has, how many obstacles are ahead and what rewards are waiting.

Firewalls

Firewalls are defensive programs that keep hackers, meaning you, out of a network. They range from a simple password to layers of encryption you have to decrypt. You beat them with your CODE skill.

Enemy Netrunners and Daemons

The Net isn't private. Other Netrunners might be there guarding the network, or as rival mercs after the same thing you want.

Daemons are powerful AI programs that can hack tech, and you. Treat them like enemy Netrunners. Luckily, when you de-rez a Daemon it's gone for good.

NET HP

Netrunners have two HP pools, IRL HP and NET HP. NET HP matches your IRL HP unless an implant changes it. Black ICE mostly hits your NET HP, but your body can still be attacked while you're under. Some Black ICE, like a Hellhound's fire, and Daemons can also damage your IRL HP straight from The Net. The INT perk Insulated Wires halves that damage, rounded down.

If your NET HP hits 0 you Flatline and go into critical condition, and you start rolling death saves. Once you stabilize, your processors are fried. You need a Ripper to repair them, and you can't access The Net until your injuries heal.

With Jack In installed, your character sheet gets a Net panel with your SHIELD and buttons that spend the RAM for SWORD, SHIELD react, SCAN Ping and Safe Jack-Out.

Black ICE

Black ICE (Intrusion Countermeasure Electronics) is defensive software built to stop Netrunners, and it can be lethal.

Slide

If a SCAN warned you that Black ICE is ahead, you can try to SLIDE past it. A SLIDE is a STEALTH check against the Black ICE's passive PERCEPTION.

Fighting Black ICE

If your SLIDE fails, or you choose to fight, the encounter starts. Black ICE moves first, and it goes right before you in the initiative order until you deal with it.

The DM tracks Black ICE in the encounter, not as NPCs. Once your crew runs into a program, it shows up in the Black ICE tab of your Codex.

Black ICE programs

Forced jack-out. You're kicked out of The Net right away. Disable safe jack-out. You can't jack out safely while it's active.

Cyberware and RipperDocs

Chrome goes into body slots. Every piece adds to your cyberware threshold. Only chrome designed for it adds RAM.

Getting chrome

New chrome comes from the DM's cyberware deck. When the DM gives you a draw, a face-down deck shows up on your Party tab. Tap Draw a card to pull one at random, or the DM can hand you a specific card.

Add to inventoryThe card goes in your backpack as cyberware. A RipperDoc still has to install it.
Put it backThe card goes back into the deck for someone else.

Cards you keep leave the deck for good, unless the DM sends one back out. Nobody but the DM knows how many cards are left.

Shards

Shards are data sticks you slot into one of your two Shard Slots. The DM decides what each one does, like a skill boost, a language or intel. Anyone can slot or pull a shard without a RipperDoc, and it doesn't count against your cyberware threshold. The effect lasts while it's slotted. Pull it out and the effect ends and the shard goes back in your bag.

Cyberware threshold

Every piece of cyberware has a cost, set by the DM or by the Fabricator who built it. Installed chrome fills your tally. Your threshold is 10 + your Cyberware skill bonus, which is your TEC mod plus your proficiency bonus if you're proficient in Cyberware (twice it if you doubled the skill). It goes up on its own as you level and raise TEC. Anything past your threshold is overflow. Overflow can only go up to half your threshold, rounded up, which sets your max. The last overflow hex is red. Fill it and you hit the point of no return. See Cyberpsychosis.

Example. TEC 14 (+2) and proficient in Cyberware at level 1 (+2) gives a threshold of 14, so overflow caps at 7 and your max is 21. A tally of 16 is 2 points of overflow, so your Cyberpsychosis save DC is 14. A tally of 21 fills the red hex.

Only a merc with the TEC Legend perk RipperDoc can install or remove cyberware, on themselves or anyone else. If nobody in the crew is a RipperDoc, you'll need to find one!

During a live session a RipperDoc sees an install panel on every party member's card. The part has to be in the patient's bag, so hand it over first. Any merc can hand items and eddies to the rest of the crew.

Hot-swappable parts

For an extra fee, a RipperDoc can make a part hot-swappable. Anyone can swap a hot part in or out of themselves without a RipperDoc, but it comes with a cost.

Status Effects and Death Saves

The DM can put status effects on your merc from the Live Session. They show in your Vitals with a round countdown if they have one. When a round ends, the DM hits End round and every timer ticks down.

Death saves

At 0 HP or 0 Net HP you start rolling death saves. Roll a d20. A 10 or higher passes. Three passes and whatever dropped you comes back at 1, so 0 Net HP comes back as 1 Net HP. Coming back from 0 Net HP also leaves your processors fried, so no Net until a Ripper repairs you. If both are at 0, both come back at 1. Three fails and you're dead, no matter which one dropped you.

Your Vitals show it as a seesaw. Three live skulls sit on the left and three dead skulls on the right. Each pass lights a live skull cyan, each fail turns a dead skull red, and the beam tips toward whichever side is winning. Any healing above 0 HP resets them.

The effects

The 5e conditions work like 5e. The cyber ones are drafts until the GM writes them.

Every item takes up a shape on the grid, like 4 × 1 for a rifle or 1 × 1 for a grenade. The GM tells you how big something is. A stack of the same item shares one spot.

Equipped weapons and worn armor don't use backpack space. Installed cyberware and vehicles don't either.

If something doesn't fit, it goes on the floor and you're Encumbered until you make room. The GM decides what that costs you.

On your sheet, tap an item to pick it up, then tap an empty space to put it there. You can also drag it, rotate it, drop it on the floor or drag it onto your equipped slots.

Item Tiers

Every weapon, armor piece, cyberware, piece of gear and consumable is Tier 1 to 4. Higher tiers hit harder and carry effects. A Fabricator can push an item up one tier at a time.

COOL Tokens

The DM hands out COOL Tokens whenever they want, like inspiration in 5e. There's no cap on how many you can hold, and using one is free.

Whenever anyone rolls a d20, you can spend a COOL Token to help or hinder that roll. The token adds your COOL mod to the roll, whether it's yours, an ally's or an enemy's.

  • With a positive COOL mod you can turn a failure into a success for you or your allies.
  • With a negative COOL mod you can turn an enemy's success into a failure.

Example. The team's Netrunner has +3 COOL. An ally rolls to hit and misses by 2. The Netrunner spends a COOL Token and adds +3 to the attack, and it hits.

If you have the COO perk You Ready to Rock?!, spending a token also gives every ally its COOL buffs and one free ACTION.

The hack list is a draft until the GM finalizes hacking rules.

Cyberpsychosis

Everything has a limit, and so does your body. There's only so much cybernetic enhancement a nervous system can handle. This limit is represented by your Cyberware Threshold and your Overflow. See Cyberware for Threshold rules.

SaveRoll 1d20 + your TEC mod against the DC
DC10, plus 2 for every point in your Cyberware Overflow
Point of No ReturnThere's an upper limit where your body can take no more, shown as a red hexagon in your Cyberware Threshold bar. Push past your Overflow into that red and it's AUTOMATIC Cyberpsychosis. Your merc is lost for good

Example. 2 points of Overflow makes the DC 14. Roll 1d20 + TEC and meet or beat 14.

A few perks help. Theseus (BOD) gives advantage on the save. Tranquility (COO) adds your COOL mod to your own and your allies' saves. Limit Setter (TEC) means the first piece of cyberware that pushes you past your threshold doesn't make you a Cyberpsycho. I'm Special (BOD) halves the Overflow penalty, so each point adds +1 to the DC instead of +2. The red hex is still automatic.

Your sheet shows the current DC and has a button to roll the save whenever you're in overflow.

Perk Trees

Every perk tree is tied to one stat. If that stat is under 10, the tree is offline. Higher tiers open as the stat climbs, and you spend 1 perk point per perk.
